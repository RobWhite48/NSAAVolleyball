How the playoffs work
1. Subdistrict
Our group of 6 teams plays a single-elimination tournament. Only the winner moves on. We’re the #2 seed.
2. District final
16 teams: the 10 subdistrict winners plus the 6 best of the teams that lost, ranked by points average. They are paired 1 v 16, 2 v 15, down to 8 v 9. The higher seed hosts. One match, win or go home.
3. State
The 8 district-final winners go to state in Lincoln, Nov 4 to 7.
How the points average works

Every match earns points, even a loss. The amount depends on the other team’s win rate. Our average is total points divided by games played. Playing a bigger class adds bonus points (+2 for Class B, +3 for Class A).

Two surprises for parents: a win over a weak team can lower our average if it’s worth less than what we already have, and points are recalculated as opponents’ records change, so our average can move even when we don’t play. That’s why we root for strong teams to win.

Words you’ll hear
Seed
Our rank in the 16-team district-final field, from our points average.
Host
Seeds 1 through 8 play at home in the district final. Seeds 9 through 16 travel.
Wildcard
A team that lost its subdistrict but is one of the 6 best by average, so it still reaches the district final.
Official vs. projected
Records, averages, brackets and times come from NSAA. Seeds, home or road, and opponents are our projections until NSAA posts the district matchups.
How we calculate the projections

Our scenario engine uses NSAA’s published rules and your team’s real schedule data. It re-scores every team’s games as records change, picks the 16-team field, and seeds it by average. For games we can’t control, it assumes the team with the better record wins, which is why we show “what if” results instead of odds.

Not modeled: playoff games in other classes that could change an opponent’s record, and any NSAA tie-break details. Always check NSAA for the official word.
